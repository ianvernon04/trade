Options 101 — learn before you trade

start here if calls, puts, and greeks are new to you

1 · What an option actually is

An option is a contract on 100 shares of a stock. A call is the right to buy at a fixed price (the strike) before a date (the expiry); you buy calls when you expect the stock to go up. A put is the right to sell at the strike; you buy puts when you expect the stock to go down. The price you pay is the premium — and it is the most you can lose on a long option.

Example: NVDA at $180. A $190 call for $5.00 costs $500 (×100). If NVDA hits $200 before expiry the option is worth ≥ $10.00 (+100%). If NVDA stays under $190 at expiry, it expires worthless (-100%). Most traders sell the option before expiry rather than exercising it.

2 · The greeks (this app computes them for you)

Delta (Δ) — how much the option moves per $1 move in the stock, and roughly the market's odds it expires in the money. A 0.35-delta call gains ~$0.35 per $1 rally.

Theta (Θ) — daily time decay. A theta of -0.05 means the option loses ~$5/day per contract just from time passing. Decay accelerates in the last 3 weeks — that's why the app prefers 30-45 DTE.

Gamma — how fast delta itself changes; Vega — sensitivity to implied volatility (1 vol point ≈ vega in $).

3 · Implied volatility & IV crush

IV is the market's forecast of how much the stock will move — it's what makes options cheap or expensive. The expected move tile on the Options tab converts IV into a ± dollar range for the chosen expiry.

IV crush: before earnings, IV (and premiums) inflate; the moment results are out, IV collapses. You can call the direction correctly and still lose money holding a long option through earnings. That's exactly what the app's ⚠ earnings warnings protect you from.

4 · Beginner-friendly strategies (in order)

Long call / long put — simplest way to trade direction; risk = premium paid.

Cash-secured put — get paid premium for agreeing to buy a stock you want anyway, at a lower price.

Covered call — own 100 shares, sell a call against them for income.

Vertical spreads (buy one option, sell another at a different strike) — cap both risk and reward, and blunt IV/theta. The right upgrade once long options make sense — especially when the app flags elevated IV.

5 · Risk rules that keep you alive

· Risk 1-2% of your account per trade, max. Position size = account × 1% ÷ premium.
· Never hold long options through earnings unless the earnings bet IS the plan.
· Take partials at +50-100%; don't round-trip winners to zero.
· Avoid options under 21 DTE while learning — theta eats you.
· Avoid illiquid chains: wide bid/ask spreads (>10% of mid) are a hidden tax.
· Journal every trade (Journal tab) — your own stats are the best teacher.

6 · Common beginner mistakes

· Buying far-OTM weekly "lottery tickets" (delta < 0.10).
· Confusing a cheap price with a cheap option (check IV first).
· Oversizing because premiums look small — one contract controls 100 shares.
· Averaging down on a losing option (theta makes it worse than stock).
· Trading the open (9:30-10:00 ET) when spreads are widest.
· No exit plan. Decide the stop and target before entry — the Agent brief writes one for you.
